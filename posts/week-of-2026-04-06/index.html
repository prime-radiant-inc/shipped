hearthstone learns Drive and PIN sign-in, a methodology proves itself

Week of April 6, 2026 · Apr 6, 2026–Apr 12, 2026 · published Apr 12, 2026

Week two was about depth. hearthstone kept its blistering pace — 116 commits — adding Google Drive document import, PIN-and-QR sign-in, multi-household and multi-owner support, dark mode, and a first cloud deploy. iterative-development grew from last week’s scaffold into a genuine spec-driven methodology — an extraction pipeline, parallel adversarial review, quality gates, and a behavior-evidence redesign — while a companion repo dogfooded the whole thing by building a working macOS dictation app across twenty-one iterations. Around them, superpowers-chrome taught its browser agent human-like mouse and keyboard control, and private-journal-mcp cut a 2.0.

prime-radiant-inc/hearthstone

A household knowledge assistant — a retrieval service over your own documents, with an iOS client and a guest-sharing model.

116 commits +22,040 −2,457

116 commits carried hearthstone from prototype toward something you could hand to a family. Google Drive integration landed end to end — an OAuth flow that returns to a custom hearthstone:// URL scheme, a Drive file picker in the iOS app, and server-side docx-to-Markdown conversion. Authentication was reworked around PINs: a unified PIN-redemption endpoint, a CLI that generates owner PINs, and QR codes an owner can scan to onboard a guest (with an AVFoundation scanner in the app). The data model opened up to multiple households and multiple owners — a household_members table, owner invite/list/remove endpoints, and a sidebar to switch between houses. The interface gained dark mode through a ResolvedTheme migration across every view, plus lightweight chat persistence. And it left the laptop: a Fly.io deploy, a TestFlight upload script, and optional OpenTelemetry tracing to Honeycomb. Sole contributor: Matt Windbrook.

prime-radiant-inc/iterative-development

A Claude Code plugin encoding an iterative, spec-driven development methodology.

72 commits +15,538 −1,867

Seventy-two commits turned last week’s scaffold into a full methodology. An extraction pipeline chunks a spec, dispatches parallel subagents, and aggregates deduplicated stories. “Parallel adversarial review” (PAR) pits a competitive auditor against each work product. Quality gates verify that every roadmap item cites a requirement and flag scope creep. An implementing-tasks skill forked in a two-stage review cycle — spec-compliance, then code-quality — and the orchestrator gained autonomy, an interrupt protocol, and crash resumption. The week closed on a “behavior evidence” redesign that reframes the whole pipeline around provable scenarios and proof obligations rather than prose requirements. Sole contributor: Jesse Vincent.
