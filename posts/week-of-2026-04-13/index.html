gauntlet reaches v1.5, hearthstone grows an admin and locks down

Week of April 13, 2026 · Apr 13, 2026–Apr 19, 2026 · published Apr 19, 2026

Two heavyweights defined the week. gauntlet landed its v1.5 transition — server-owned runs, a real configuration system, and per-run browser isolation — across sixty-nine commits. hearthstone matured on the operations side: an admin console, house deletion with proper 410 Gone semantics, token-bucket rate limiting, a security pass that stopped leaking owner PINs, and a relicense to Apache 2.0. Elsewhere, a new kindle-highlight-exporter turned Kindle annotations into Markdown, and superpowers built tooling to mirror itself as a Codex plugin.

Featured

prime-radiant-inc/gauntlet

An AI-powered QA testing framework.

69 commits +17,826 −3,648

Sixty-nine commits drove the v1.5 transition. Runs became server-owned — an ActiveRunRegistry tracks in-flight runs, execution detaches behind a 202, and a fresh WebSocket connection receives a snapshot on open — with the UI rebuilt around URL-driven live runs and run IDs as the primary identity. A real configuration system landed alongside: an AppConfig type with precedence and source tracking, a gauntlet config subcommand, a /api/config/effective inspection endpoint, and loud rejection of unknown CLI flags. v1.5 itself brought a context module, per-run browser isolation, profiles with passkey support, and hardened HTTP/CLI boundary validation. Matt Windbrook drove the week; Simon Willison contributed two commits.

prime-radiant-inc/hearthstone

A household knowledge assistant — a retrieval service over your own documents, with an iOS client and a guest-sharing model.

70 commits +16,286 −3,484

Seventy commits shifted hearthstone toward operability and safety. A shareable join flow arrived — join_urls, a /join/:pin landing page, and QR codes that encode them — alongside an admin console (a boot-minted admin token, a requireAdmin middleware, and an admin UI) for managing houses. House deletion was built out carefully: cascade deletes, 410 Gone for removed houses (which the app detects and prunes from its sidebar), and a last-owner guard. A token-bucket rate limiter landed with tiered limits, Fly/XFF client-IP resolution, and an admin panel for live throttles. A security pass scrubbed placeholder emails from API responses and stopped leaking owner PINs to an external QR-code service by rendering QR codes server-side. And the project relicensed to Apache 2.0. Sole contributor: Matt Windbrook.
